1What this menu now accounts for

The dietary briefs are unchanged; the four new details change how the lunch is bought, cooked and served.

Host A — one of the hosts

No sugar, yeast, wheat, dairy, vinegar — or "foods causing cancer".

Stage 4 breast cancer. Every shared dish is free of all six, with no processed or cured meat, charred cooking, refined sugar or ultra-processed ingredients.

Guest B

Eats chicken, and no other meat.

Chicken is the main for all ten. No bacon, suet, beef stock, anchovies, lard or gelatine in the shared dishes.

Three guests

A request for tongue.

Red meat at the table is fine. One slow-poached tongue on a separate, labelled platter with its own knife and tongs, at one end of the table — apart so only the three who asked are served it.

Recovering alcoholic

One guest.

The whole table is alcohol-free — no wine, beer, brandy custard, vanilla extract or alcohol in cooking. One drinks menu for everyone, so nobody is singled out.

Port Alfred

Eastern Cape coast.

Small town, hot and humid. Order the tongue ahead, bring specialty items from Gqeberha or East London, use the pantry swaps, keep cold food in a cooler.

Both hosts (including Host A)

Both in their 80s.

Softer textures, finely chopped nuts, paper-thin tongue, light seasoning, an earlier sitting, large-print labels and short service. You do setup and clearing.

You are cooking

One cook, one oven.

Almost everything is make-ahead. The oven works in two batches, there is a clock-based schedule, and you bring your own kit.

Please read. "Foods causing cancer" is not a single defined list. I have read it as the categories most consistently flagged in cancer-prevention guidance — processed and cured meats, alcohol, charred meat, added or refined sugar, and heavily processed foods — and built the shared menu from whole, fresh ingredients. This is a menu, not medical advice or a treatment claim. Please confirm the final ingredient list with Host A and her oncology dietitian, check every packaged label, and quietly check with both hosts (Host A included) about medication-related diets (for example warfarin/vitamin K, low salt or diabetes) and any chewing or swallowing considerations.
On the alcohol-free table. I have also left out alcohol-free beers and wines, which some people in recovery prefer not to have around — easy to add back if your guest is happy with them. There is no need to explain the absence of wine: the alcohol-free drinks menu is simply what is being served to all ten.

2The menu

Everything marked shared is safe for all ten, including Host A and the chicken-only guest. Lemon and lime do the work vinegar normally would.

On arrival

Shared · alcohol-free

First course

Shared · all ten
  • Chilled fennel, cucumber & avocado saladBaby leaves, thinly sliced radish, dill or parsley and finely ground toasted sunflower seeds, dressed with lemon juice, extra-virgin olive oil, black pepper and sea salt. Shred the fennel and slice the cucumber thin. No vinegar, mustard or honey.

The main table

Shared · all ten
  • Rosemary, lemon & garlic roast chicken10 boneless chicken thighs or supremes (~180 g each) — easier to serve and to chew than three whole birds. Olive oil rather than butter, no sugar glaze, no wine, no stock cubes, no charred edges.
  • Lemon & parsley pan gravyHomemade chicken stock reduced with lemon and herbs, thickened with cornflour (Maizena). No flour, yeast extract, wine or cream.
  • Olive oil & rosemary roast baby potatoesCrisp outside, soft inside. No butter, no duck fat.
  • Brown rice & lentil pilafHerbs, lemon zest and spring onion, cooked in homemade vegetable stock — no cubes. Quinoa and wild rice are lovely if you can get them; brown rice and lentils are the reliable local version.
  • Silky cauliflower puréeCauliflower, garlic, olive oil and a pinch of nutmeg, blended until creamy. No cream, butter or cheese.
  • Roasted green beans, baby carrots & courgettesWith thyme and lemon, cooked until tender rather than al dente.
  • Mushroom, leek & celery "stuffing" bakeBound with rice, baked in its own dish. No bread, yeast, sausage, bacon or suet.
  • Fresh tomato, roast pepper & basil relishThe sugar-free, vinegar-free stand-in for cranberry sauce.

7One-cook schedule (for a 13:00 sitting)

The point of the make-ahead day is that on Christmas morning you only handle the chicken, potatoes, vegetables, gravy and the final salad toss.

  • A week aheadOrder the tongue. Buy or order specialty items. Confirm Host A's list with her dietitian. Keep smoked gammon quantity/source/label and the homemade ice-cream recipe marked as pending. Quietly check with both hosts (Host A included) about medication-related diets, salt/sugar, chewing or swallowing, and whether a fully alcohol-free table suits the guest in recovery.
  • Two days aheadCheck every label, including the pudding, mustard, rice cakes, any vanilla flavouring, drink mixers, any recipe-supplied ice-cream ingredients and any smoked gammon packaging once the source is confirmed. Write large-print label cards for the tongue, smoked gammon and recipe-pending ice-cream. Pack your kit.
  • Day before · make-ahead dayPoach the tongue for about 4 hours; cool, peel and refrigerate in its own sealed container. Make chicken and vegetable stocks (no cubes). Make the white bean dip and tomato relish. Roast and finely chop the nuts. Bake the pears. Make the alcohol-free, suet-free pudding. Do not prepare the homemade ice-cream or smoked gammon yet — recipe/preparation and labels are still pending. Wash and dry salad leaves, cut vegetables, store in sealed containers. Chill the drinks.
  • 09:30Preheat the oven to 200 °C. Chicken and potatoes in: chicken on the top shelf, potatoes below. One oven, first batch.
  • 10:15Chicken out to rest. Vegetables and the stuffing bake go in (second batch). Reheat the pilaf and cauliflower purée on the stovetop.
  • 10:45Make the gravy from the pan juices, homemade stock and a cornflour slurry.
  • 11:30Vegetables out; warm the pears briefly if you like. Slice the tongue paper-thin on its own board with its own knife; warm gently in its own pan. If smoked gammon preparation details have since been supplied, prepare and slice it last on its own board with its own knife and tongs. Wash hands and change utensils before returning to shared dishes.
  • 12:15Dress the salad with lemon. Put out platters, large-print labels and separate tongs; set the separate tongue and smoked gammon platters apart, and the extras sideboard at the far end. Hold the recipe-pending ice-cream off the shared table until cleared. Keep cold dishes in the cooler until the last minute.
  • 12:45Slice the chicken, pour the gravy into a labelled jug, fill the drinks jugs.
  • 13:00Serve: salad first, then the main platters, then the cleared desserts. Hold the recipe-pending ice-cream until its recipe, labels and dietary clearance are supplied. Keep the sitting comfortable in length. You do the clearing.

8Serving plan, so nothing crosses

  1. One shared table, entirely alcohol-free; only the tongue platter, smoked gammon platter and extras sideboard are "for some". Do not explain the absence of wine — the alcohol-free drinks menu is simply what is being served.
  2. Prep the tongue and smoked gammon last, each on its own separate board with its own knife and tongs; wash hands and change utensils before returning to the shared dishes.
  3. Every platter gets its own serving spoon or tongs and a large-print label card — "Shared · safe for all", "Tongue · for 3", "Smoked gammon · not for Host A or Guest B", or "Ice-cream · recipe pending".
  4. Set the tongue and smoked gammon platters apart, each with its own label, board, knife and tongs. Never pass shared gravy, or a shared serving spoon, over either platter — the shared dishes stay clean for all ten.
  5. Keep mustard, pickles, the sugar bowl, the salt bowl and the sweetened spritzer on the sideboard; the unsweetened jug stays on the table for everyone.
  6. For both hosts (including Host A): put everything within reach, pass platters or plate for them rather than asking them to get up, offer smaller portions, and keep seasoning gentle with salt available at the table.
