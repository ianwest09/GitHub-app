# Christmas Lunch for Ten

*Port Alfred, Eastern Cape · a summer table in the shade.*
*One menu everyone can share — plus tongue for three. Cooked by one person, served alcohol-free.*
**10 guests · 4 courses · 2 hosts in their 80s · a fully alcohol-free table**

---

## What this menu now accounts for

| Consideration | What it means for the menu |
| --- | --- |
| **Guest A — stage 4 breast cancer** | No sugar, yeast, wheat, dairy, vinegar, or "foods causing cancer". Every shared dish is free of all six. No processed or cured meat, charred cooking, refined sugar or ultra-processed ingredients. |
| **Guest B — chicken only** | Chicken is the shared main. No bacon, suet, beef stock, anchovies, lard or gelatine anywhere in the shared dishes. |
| **3 guests — tongue** | Red meat at the table is fine. One separate, labelled platter with its own knife and tongs, at one end of the table — kept apart so only the three who asked are served it, and so the shared dishes stay clean for the chicken-only guest. |
| **1 guest — recovering alcoholic** | **The whole table is alcohol-free.** No wine, beer, brandy custard, rum-soaked fruit, wine in the gravy or poaching liquid, or vanilla extract. Nothing is cooked with alcohol and no one is singled out — everyone gets the same alcohol-free drinks menu. I have also left out alcohol-free beer/wine look-alikes, which some people in recovery prefer to avoid; easy to add back if your guest is happy with them. |
| **Port Alfred, Eastern Cape** | A small coastal town: specialty ingredients may not be on shelves. Order the tongue ahead, bring hard-to-find items from Gqeberha or East London, and use the pantry swaps below. Christmas here is hot, humid and often windy — keep cold dishes in a cooler, serve in the shade. |
| **Two hosts in their 80s** | Softer textures, finely chopped nuts, paper-thin tongue, gently cooked vegetables, seasoning kept light with salt at the table, an earlier sitting, large-print labels and short service. You do the setup and the clearing. |
| **You are cooking** | Almost everything is make-ahead, the oven works in two batches only, and there is a clock-based schedule below. Bring your own kit if you are working in the hosts' kitchen. |

> **Please read.** "Foods causing cancer" is not a single defined list. I have interpreted it as the categories most consistently flagged in cancer-prevention guidance — processed and cured meats, alcohol, charred meat, added/refined sugar and heavily processed foods — and built the shared menu from whole, fresh ingredients. Nothing here is medical advice or a treatment claim. Please confirm the final ingredient list with Guest A and her oncology dietitian, check every packaged label, and quietly check with the hosts about any medication-related diets (for example warfarin/vitamin K, low salt or diabetes) and any chewing or swallowing considerations.

## On arrival — *shared · alcohol-free*

- **Lemon–herb white bean dip** (white beans, lemon, garlic, olive oil, parsley — no tahini needed) with soft vegetable batons and lettuce cups; plain rice cakes or 100%-rice crackers, label-checked for no wheat, yeast or sugar. In doubt, just use more lettuce cups.
- **Rosemary roasted almonds and pumpkin seeds, finely chopped** for easy chewing — or swap for soft avocado and cucumber cups.
- **Drinks, one menu for everyone:** unsweetened iced rooibos with mint and lemon, cucumber–lemon water, sparkling water with citrus. A small, lightly sweetened granadilla & lime spritzer jug lives on the extras sideboard for those who want it; Guest A's jug stays unsweetened. No wine, no beer, no alcohol-free look-alikes.

## First course — *shared*

- **Chilled fennel, cucumber & avocado salad** — baby leaves, thinly sliced radish, dill or parsley, finely ground toasted sunflower seeds, dressed with lemon juice, extra-virgin olive oil, black pepper and sea salt. Finely shred the fennel and slice the cucumber thin so it is easy to eat. No vinegar, mustard or honey.

## The main table — *shared, all ten*

- **Rosemary, lemon & garlic roast chicken** — 10 boneless chicken thighs or supremes (~180 g each), easier to serve and to chew than three whole birds. Olive oil rather than butter, no sugar glaze, no wine, no stock cubes, no charred edges.
- **Lemon & parsley pan gravy** — homemade chicken stock reduced with lemon and herbs, thickened with **cornflour (Maizena)**. No flour, yeast extract, wine or cream.
- **Olive oil & rosemary roast baby potatoes** — crisp outside, soft inside. No butter, no duck fat.
- **Brown rice & lentil pilaf** — herbs, lemon zest and spring onion, cooked in homemade vegetable stock (no cubes). Quinoa and wild rice are lovely if you can get them, but brown rice and lentils are the reliable local version.
- **Silky cauliflower purée** — cauliflower, garlic, olive oil and a pinch of nutmeg, blended until creamy. No cream, butter or cheese.
- **Roasted green beans, baby carrots & courgettes** with thyme and lemon, cooked until tender rather than al dente.
- **Mushroom, leek & celery "stuffing" bake** — bound with rice, baked in its own dish. No bread, yeast, sausage, bacon or suet.
- **Fresh tomato, roast pepper & basil relish** — the sugar-free, vinegar-free stand-in for cranberry sauce.

## For the three who asked — *separate platter, separate utensils*

- **Slow-poached beef/ox tongue** — 1 large tongue (~1.5 kg), simmered about 4 hours with bay leaves, peppercorns, onion and celery (no wine, no vinegar brine), peeled and sliced **paper-thin**, served warm. Order it from the butcher a week ahead.
- **Parsley, lemon & olive oil salsa** — fresh herbs, lemon zest, garlic and olive oil. No vinegar, no mustard, no brined capers.
- Beef tongue is red meat/offal and only three people eat it, so it has its own platter, board, knife and tongs: it never touches the chicken, the shared gravy or the shared serving spoons. Red meat at the table is perfectly fine — the separation is simply about keeping the shared dishes clean for everyone and respecting the chicken-only guest. A small pot of mustard and pickles can go on the extras sideboard for these three, clearly labelled, with its own spoon.

## Dessert — *shared*

- **Coconut rice pots** — rice simmered in coconut milk with cinnamon and a vanilla **pod** (not vanilla extract), layered with berries and toasted coconut. Soft, no added sugar, dairy, wheat or gelatine.
- **Cinnamon-baked pears with finely chopped toasted pecans**, baked until very soft — sweetened only by the fruit itself.
- To finish: unsweetened rooibos, mint tea, coffee (decaf available). The sugar bowl lives on the extras sideboard, not on the shared table.

## The optional extras sideboard — *for the nine · alcohol-free*

- **Christmas pudding made without brandy or rum** — moisten with rooibos or orange juice instead. Suet-free (butter-based, so no beef fat for the chicken-only guest), with custard.
- Mustard, pickles and chutneys for the tongue.
- Sugar bowl, salt bowl, and the lightly sweetened spritzer jug.
- **No wine, no beer, no alcohol-free look-alikes, no alcohol-based flavourings anywhere in the lunch.**

## Hidden-ingredient checklist

Take this to the shop. These are the ones that catch people out.

| Avoid | Often hides in |
| --- | --- |
| **Sugar** (also honey, maple, agave, coconut sugar, dextrose, maltose, syrups) | Glazes, cranberry sauce, cured meats, stock powders, crackers, spice blends, chutneys, desserts, soft drinks |
| **Yeast** (also yeast extract, brewer's yeast, nutritional yeast, hydrolysed protein) | Stock cubes, gravy granules, bouillon, Marmite, soy sauce, Worcestershire sauce, bread, beer, some spice blends |
| **Wheat** | Bread, stuffing, couscous, pasta, soy sauce, gravy thickener, malt vinegar, some crackers and spice blends |
| **Dairy** | Butter, ghee, cream, cheese, yoghurt, custard, many margarines and chocolates, some stock powders |
| **Vinegar** (all kinds, incl. balsamic and malt) | Vinaigrettes, mustard, mayonnaise, ketchup, hot sauce, Worcestershire, pickles, relishes, brined capers and olives, sauerkraut |
| **Other meats** (for Guest B) | Bacon in stuffing, suet in pudding, beef stock, anchovies/Worcestershire, lard, gelatine |
| **Charred bits & alcohol** | Braai marks, burnt roast edges, wine in gravy, brandy in sauces |
| **Alcohol** (for the recovering-alcoholic guest) | Brandy or rum in Christmas pudding, mince pies and custard; wine in gravy and poaching liquid; beer in batters; vanilla and other flavour extracts; liqueur-soaked dried fruit; some mustards; wine vinegar; kombucha and "non-alcoholic" beers (which can contain up to 0.5%) |
| **Hard or whole nuts/seeds** (for the hosts) | Roasted almonds, pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds, toasted pecans — chop or grind them finely |

**Five easy swaps:** **lemon or lime juice** anywhere you would normally reach for vinegar · **cornflour** instead of flour · **olive oil** instead of butter · **homemade stock** instead of cubes · **a vanilla pod and rooibos** instead of vanilla extract and brandy.

## Sourcing in Port Alfred

Port Alfred is a small town on the Eastern Cape Sunshine Coast. Order anything unusual ahead, or bring it from Gqeberha (Port Elizabeth) or East London.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Beef/ox tongue | Order from the local butcher a week ahead. If unavailable, bring one chilled from a butcher in Gqeberha or East London. |
| Quinoa, wild rice, chia, tahini, arrowroot, pomegranate, fresh tarragon | Often not stocked. Bring them if you want them, or use the swaps: brown rice + lentils, cornflour, vanilla pod, coconut milk, local berries, parsley/rosemary/thyme/mint. |
| Rice cakes / rice crackers | Check labels for wheat, yeast and sugar. If in doubt, use lettuce cups and soft vegetable batons. |
| Coconut milk, canned white beans, cornflour (Maizena), rooibos, brown rice, lentils | Reliable South African pantry staples. |
| Chicken, potatoes, cauliflower, green beans, carrots, courgettes, mushrooms, leeks, celery, tomatoes, peppers, lemons, garlic, herbs | Standard supermarket and greengrocer. |
| Fresh fish | Port Alfred has excellent seafood, but it is deliberately left off: the "chicken and no other meat" brief makes it ambiguous. |

Hot, humid and often windy at Christmas: keep cold dishes in a cooler with ice packs until serving, serve in the shade, and do not leave the dip or coconut dishes out in the heat for more than an hour. Resist the braai for the shared dishes — charred meat is off Guest A's plate.

## One-cook schedule (for a 13:00 sitting)

The point of the make-ahead day is that on Christmas morning you only handle the chicken, potatoes, vegetables, gravy and the final salad toss.

- **A week ahead** — Order the tongue. Buy or order specialty items. Confirm Guest A's list with her dietitian. Quietly check with the hosts about medication-related diets, salt/sugar, chewing or swallowing, and whether a fully alcohol-free table suits the recovering-alcoholic guest (it is the safest default).
- **Two days ahead** — Check every label, including the pudding, mustard, rice cakes, vanilla and drink mixers. Write the large-print label cards. Pack your kit.
- **Day before (make-ahead day)** — Poach the tongue for about 4 hours; cool, peel and refrigerate in its own sealed container. Make chicken and vegetable stocks (no cubes). Make the white bean dip and the tomato relish. Roast and finely chop the nuts. Simmer and portion the coconut rice pots. Bake the pears. Make the alcohol-free, suet-free pudding. Wash and dry salad leaves, cut vegetables, store in sealed containers. Chill the drinks.
- **09:30** — Preheat the oven to 200 °C. Chicken and potatoes in: chicken on the top shelf, potatoes below. One oven, first batch.
- **10:15** — Chicken out to rest. Vegetables and the stuffing bake go in (second batch). Reheat the pilaf and cauliflower purée on the stovetop.
- **10:45** — Make the gravy from the pan juices, homemade stock and a cornflour slurry.
- **11:30** — Vegetables out; warm the pears briefly if you like. Slice the tongue paper-thin on its own board with its own knife; warm gently in its own pan. Wash hands and change utensils before returning to shared dishes.
- **12:15** — Dress the salad with lemon. Put out platters, large-print labels and separate tongs; set the extras sideboard at the far end. Keep cold dishes in the cooler until the last minute.
- **12:45** — Slice the chicken, pour the gravy into a labelled jug, fill the drinks jugs.
- **13:00** — Serve: salad first, then the main platters, then dessert. Keep the sitting comfortable in length. You do the clearing.

## Serving plan (so nothing crosses)

1. One shared table, entirely alcohol-free; only the tongue platter and the extras sideboard are "for some". Do not explain the absence of wine — the alcohol-free drinks menu is simply what is being served.
2. Prep the tongue last, on a separate board with a separate knife; wash hands and change utensils before returning to the shared dishes.
3. Every platter gets its own serving spoon or tongs and a **large-print** label card — "Shared · safe for all" or "Tongue · for 3".
4. Set the tongue platter at one end of the table with its own label and tongs. Never pass shared gravy, or a shared serving spoon, over it — the shared dishes stay clean for all ten.
5. Keep mustard, pickles, the sugar bowl, the salt bowl and the sweetened spritzer on the sideboard; the unsweetened jug stays on the table for everyone.
6. For the hosts: put everything within reach, pass platters or plate for them rather than asking them to get up, offer smaller portions, and keep seasoning gentle with salt available at the table.

## Two quick things to confirm

- **With Guest A and her oncology dietitian:** the final ingredient list and every packaged label. Red meat at the table is settled — the tongue platter stays.
- **With the hosts:** any medication-related diet needs (warfarin/vitamin K, low salt, diabetes), any chewing/swallowing or denture considerations, and — discreetly — whether the fully alcohol-free plan suits the guest in recovery. It is the safest default and nobody needs to be singled out.
